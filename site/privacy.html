StreamsPlayer.

Privacy Policy for StreamsPlayer

Last updated: November 10, 2026

StreamsPlayer is a player for internet radio, live video (HLS, DASH) and RTSP streams that can also broadcast live from the microphone and the camera. It is an Android app for phones, an app for Wear OS watches and a watch face, published by Serhii Zhyhunenko (SerZhyAle). This policy covers all three packages and says which one it means where they differ.

In short:

  • The developer receives none of your data. The developer runs no server, keeps no accounts and the app contains no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking code.
  • Everything optional is off until you turn it on: the exchange server, Google Drive, and publishing a broadcast through a server.
  • What leaves your device goes to a place you chose: the stream you play, the server you named, your own Google account, the listeners you gave an address to.

1. What the app does not do

  • It has no telemetry: no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no advertising identifier and no third-party tracking library. The libraries it is built from are listed in section 10.
  • It creates no account. An account on an exchange server is made elsewhere (section 9); this app only signs in.
  • It writes no log file in the version you install, and sends no diagnostics anywhere.
  • It does not ask for your contacts, your location, your photos or files, or your phone number (section 4 lists every permission).
  • It does not record broadcasts and keeps no copy of what it plays or sends.

2. What stays on your device

  • Your favorites, pins, collections, your own channels, settings and playback state are stored in the app's private storage. The app is excluded from Android backup (allowBackup="false"), so they are not copied to your Google account or to another device by Android.
  • Exchange sign-in tokens, and the passwords you save for protected broadcasts, are encrypted with a key that never leaves Android Keystore (AES-256-GCM). The password you type to sign in is wiped from memory right after it is used and is never written to disk or to a log.
  • The channel catalog and the channel pictures you downloaded are stored on the device.
  • Removing the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes all of this.

3. What leaves your device, and where it goes

Each line is one thing the app can send, to whom, and when.

  • The streams you play. When you play a channel, the app connects to the server of that stream, the address you or the catalog gave it. That server sees your IP address and the usual request details. Some stream addresses use plain HTTP or RTSP, which are not encrypted; the app opens the address you ask for. The developer is not a party to these connections.
  • The channel catalog. When you choose Refresh catalog in the menu or Update on the offer of a new channel list, and on the watch also when its channel list first loads, the app downloads the catalog file from GitHub (github.com). GitHub sees your IP address and the request, as for any download. Nothing about you is sent with it.
  • Checking for a new channel list. On the phone, when you open the channel list, the app asks GitHub (github.com) whether a newer catalog file is published there: a short request that downloads no file. Only when one is, the list offers it. In Settings > General, Updating the channel list set to Only when I ask turns this check off; Automatically on Wi-Fi downloads a newer catalog without asking, on Wi-Fi and at most once a day. GitHub sees your IP address and the request. Nothing about you is sent with it.
  • Channel pictures. On the phone, only when you accept the offer that follows a catalog update; on the watch, when its channel list first loads and after Refresh catalog. The app downloads the pictures, and a small file that says which ones are current, from GitHub (github.com). GitHub sees your IP address and the request, as for any download. Nothing about you is sent with it.
  • A live broadcast on your network. While you broadcast, listeners connect straight to your phone or watch at the address it shows. This connection does not pass through any server and is not encrypted: anyone on that network who has the address can listen. It starts only when you start a broadcast and stops when you stop it.
  • The exchange server (optional). Section 5.
  • Google Drive (optional). Section 7.
  • Between your phone and your watch. The two devices exchange your pins and channels, playback and broadcast commands, and the exchange pairing (section 8) over the Wear OS Data Layer, which is part of Google Play services on your own devices.
  • QR codes. The scanner reads the camera picture on the device only; the decoded text goes back to the form that asked for it. It never opens a link or connects by itself.

5. The exchange server (optional)

An exchange server connects your own devices when they cannot reach each other directly, so a broadcast from one device can be found and played on another. It is a program that you run yourself, or that a person you know runs for you. The developer of this app runs no exchange server and offers none to the public. Nothing here is on until you sign in to a server.

  • What the app sends. At sign-in: your login and password, this device's name, a random device identifier and the public part of the device's encryption key. While connected: that the device is online, and, for each broadcast the phone starts, its title, its mode (sound or camera) and the addresses a listener can try. For the relay through the server, its content as well - encrypted on your device first (section 6). Listening to a broadcast sends the server the request to join it.
  • How it is protected. The connection to the server is encrypted (TLS). The first time a server shows a certificate the app asks you, and a certificate that later changes is refused. Your password is used only for the sign-in itself: it goes to the server you named over that encrypted connection to prove who you are, and the app does not keep it. Afterwards the app uses a token that stays in Android Keystore. Change password sends the old and the new password the same way.
  • Adding a server is the choice. Without an exchange server a broadcast stays on your own network. Once you add one, every broadcast of the phone is announced on it from its start, and the broadcast screen names the server and the state of the broadcast there. Removing the server keeps broadcasts on your network again. A guest of a server announces nothing.
  • Broadcasts of other people. Opening a shared broadcast connects to the addresses its link names, which can be another person's exchange server. That path sends no password and no token, and needs no account. The accepted certificate is kept locally for that broadcast, and a changed one is refused.
  • Casts to your own device. A broadcast sent from another device of your account asks before it plays. Playing such casts without asking is an optional setting, off by default, and works only while the app is open.
  • What the server keeps. That is decided by whoever runs the server. The server program of this app family keeps the account, its devices and the records of published broadcasts until the account is deleted, passes broadcast content on without storing it, and writes a log whose retention its operator sets and which holds no password, token or content. Broadcast identifiers work as access keys and are never written to this app's logs.
  • The developer receives none of this.

6. Encryption: what the exchange server can and cannot see

The text below is the one every program of this family gives, in these words:

The exchange server connects your devices, so it knows the names of your account, devices, folders and broadcasts, sees which devices are online, and how much is sent and when. It cannot open what goes through it: folders and their files, broadcasts and the clipboard are encrypted on your device and opened only on your other devices or by the people you gave access to. To look inside, whoever runs the server would have to add a device of their own to your account, and it would appear in your list of devices.

For this app that means:

  • Encrypted on your device, unreadable to the server: the sound and the picture of a broadcast you publish through the server, including a broadcast sent by your watch. Only your other devices of the account, and the people you gave the link, can open it.
  • Visible to the server, because without it the server connects nothing: your login, each device's name and presence, a broadcast's title and mode, who connects to whom and when, the addresses clients connect from, and the size and timing of what is sent.
  • No clear fallback. If a server does not support encryption, the app does not publish a broadcast through it.
  • Not covered: the connection on your own network (section 3), which does not use the server, and the streams you play from other people's servers.

7. Google Drive (optional)

Announcing a broadcast via Google Drive is optional and off by default. It starts only when you sign in with your own Google account on the Exchange screen and turn on Announce via Google Drive for a broadcast; the choice is remembered separately for sound and for the camera.

  • One narrow permission. The app asks Google for a single permission, https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata, a non-sensitive one. It reaches only the hidden app folder that Google keeps for this app family in your Drive. The app cannot see, list, read or change any other file in your Drive.
  • Your own account only. The folder is read and written by your own devices signed in to the same Google account. Other people are reached through an exchange server or a link, never through your Drive.
  • What an announcement holds. A small text file with the broadcast's title and mode, this phone's name and a random identifier, the start time, the addresses a listener can try (your local network address and, if you also publish through your exchange server, that server's addresses) and a lifetime stamp. A second small file names this phone to your other devices. Never the sound or the picture - the stream does not go through Google Drive.
  • The words of the family: "Via Google Drive, files and the clipboard are kept in your own Google account, like your other files on Google Drive; no exchange server takes part." This app uses Google Drive for announcements only. The announcement is not encrypted by this app.
  • When it is removed. The files are deleted when the broadcast stops. If the phone cannot delete them (for example, it lost the network), other devices read the announcement as ended after about 90 seconds, and the phone deletes the leftovers the next time it announces.
  • What is read. While the broadcast list is open, the app reads the announcement and device-name files that your other devices wrote to the same folder and shows them in the list under "via Google Drive". Files of a broadcast that has ended are not shown.
  • What stays on the phone. The access token is held by Google Play services; the app does not store it, log it or send it anywhere except to Google's Drive service. The e-mail address of the account is not read. The app keeps only that you signed in and a random identifier of this phone for the announcement files.
  • Signing out. Sign out of Google Drive stops the app from using Drive and does not touch your exchange account. You can also withdraw the access in your Google account settings.
  • Your storage. The files take a few kilobytes of your Google storage while a broadcast runs. The developer receives none of this data.
  • Limited use. The use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. It is used only for the announcements above, is not transferred to anyone, is not used for advertising and is not used to train any model.

8. Your watch

  • The watch app works without the phone. A watch connects to an exchange account only when you choose Connect watch on the phone's Exchange screen.
  • Connecting sends the watch the server address, certificate trust information, the login and a short-lived one-time pairing code over the Wear OS Data Layer. The watch creates its own random device identity and stores its own encrypted token. Neither the phone's password nor either device's token is transferred. The phone remembers the watch it connected so that it can remove it later.
  • The watch broadcasts sound only. When the watch is connected to an account, every broadcast is announced on that exchange server from its start, over the watch's own Wi-Fi or mobile connection; to keep broadcasts on your own network only, sign out on the phone.
  • The watch's connection to the server stays open while a broadcast runs, and otherwise only while an audio player or broadcast screen is visible and the display is on.
  • Signing out on the phone asks the watch to erase its local account and revoke its device identity. If the watch is unavailable, the phone revokes it on the server and shows that the cleanup on the watch is still pending.
  • The watch face shows the time. It asks for no permission and stores or sends nothing.

9. Accounts and deleting them

  • This app creates no account. It never registers one. An account on an exchange server is made in another program of the family or by the person who runs the server; this app signs in to it.
  • Sign out on this device revokes this device on the server, forgets the token and removes the entry from the app (and signs out the watch you connected). The channels you kept stay on the device. The account itself and your other devices stay.
  • To delete the account itself, use the program where it was made, or ask the person who runs the server: only the operator of a server can delete an account on it. The portal page "How to delete an account" describes both ways, in English, Russian and Ukrainian; the app opens it from the Exchange screen.
  • What may remain after that: the server's log for the retention period its operator set, copies of broadcasts already delivered to people who were listening, and the announcement files in your own Google Drive, which you can delete there.
  • Everything the app keeps on your device is removed by uninstalling it or clearing its storage.

10. Components from others

  • Google Play services is part of Android on your device. The app uses its Wear OS Data Layer to talk to your watch and, only if you sign in for Drive announcements, its Authorization API. Google's own terms apply to those parts. If you have allowed Android to share usage and diagnostics data, Google Play can show the developer anonymous crash and performance statistics; that is a Google facility, not code in this app, and it carries no content.
  • Open source libraries do the playback (AndroidX Media3 and ExoPlayer), networking (OkHttp), the on-device broadcast server (NanoHTTPD and RootEncoder), camera access (CameraX), QR decoding (ZXing), cryptography (Bouncy Castle), the local database (Room), dependency injection (Hilt), and background downloads (WorkManager). None of them sends data anywhere on its own. The full list with licenses is in THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt inside the app package.
  • The app carries no advertising, analytics or tracking library, and the version catalog it is built from has none.

11. Children

StreamsPlayer opens streams that other people publish and that the app does not curate, so it is not directed at children. It knowingly collects no information from anyone, children included.
